What these words mean

A plain-English reference for every term that appears above. Same definitions used in the hover tooltips.

Visitor / User
A different person who came to your site. One person who visits three times still counts as one visitor for the month.
Session / Visit
A single visit to your site. One person can have several sessions in a month — think of it like store visits, not unique customers.
Engagement rate
The percentage of visits where someone actually stuck around — at least 10 seconds, more than one page, or did something on the site. Higher is better. (This replaced the older "bounce rate," which worked the opposite way.)
Average engagement time
How long the typical visitor was actively focused on your site (not just idle in a background tab).
Key event / Inquiry / Conversion
A specific action you care about — a form submission, a phone tap, a booking, a newsletter signup. The whole point of the site, in metric form.
Conversion rate
Of all the people who visited, the percentage who completed a key event. Like a "closing rate" for your website.
Landing page
The first page someone lands on when they arrive at your site. Often not the homepage.
Organic search
Visitors who found you by searching on Google (or Bing) and clicked a regular, non-ad result. This is what SEO grows.
Direct traffic
Visitors who typed your URL or used a bookmark — or, often, clicked a link from somewhere we can't track (a text message, a PDF, certain apps).
Referral traffic
Visitors who clicked a link to you from another website (not a search engine, not social media).
Channel
A category of where visitors came from (Organic, Direct, Referral, Social, Email, Paid).
Impression
One time your site appeared in a Google search result. Even if no one clicked, it counts as exposure. Analogy: your shop sign got seen.
Click
Someone saw you in Google and clicked through to your site. Analogy: someone walked in.
Click-through rate (CTR)
Of the people who saw you in Google, the percentage who actually clicked. (Clicks ÷ impressions.) Like a batting average for your search listing.
Average position
Roughly where your site shows up on the Google results page. Lower is better — position 1 is the top result, position 11 means you're on page 2.
Query / Search term
The actual words someone typed into Google when your site showed up.
Branded query
A search that includes your business name. These are people who already know you.
Non-branded query
A search that doesn't mention your business name. These are strangers — the audience SEO is trying to reach.
Indexed page
A page on your site that Google has on file and is willing to show in search results. If a page isn't indexed, it cannot appear in Google.
AI Overview
Google's AI-generated answer that sometimes appears at the top of search results. It can reduce clicks even when you rank well, because the user gets their answer without visiting your site.
SEO audit score
A 0–100 grade for how well your site follows search engine best practices. The trend matters more than the absolute number.
Critical issue
A problem flagged by SEO Space that is actively hurting your rankings and should be fixed first.
Warning
A smaller SEO issue worth fixing but not urgent.
Tracked keyword
A specific search phrase we're monitoring to see your ranking on Google week by week.
Page 1 ranking
Your site appears in the top 10 results on Google for that phrase. Most clicks happen on page 1.
Backlink
Another website's link pointing to yours. Google treats good backlinks like recommendations — quality beats quantity. Analogy: like reviews from other businesses.
Meta title / Meta description
The clickable headline and short blurb that show up in Google search results. The "shop window" for your page.
Alt text
A short text description attached to an image. Helps Google understand the image and helps screen readers for visually impaired visitors.
